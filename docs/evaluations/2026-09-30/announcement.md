# Kition Pilot Announcement

On November 12, 2026, we will launch the fictional Kition Pilot for a focused group of small research teams (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:3). The pilot is designed around a simple promise: help teams organize evidence and drafts in one workspace (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:4). It is intentionally limited in scope, with 24 invited teams participating (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:5). Availability after the pilot has not yet been decided, so this announcement should be read as an invitation to learn with the pilot group, not as a general release notice (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:5).

The pilot reflects feedback from a synthetic survey of the invited teams. Eighteen of the 24 invited teams responded to that survey (source://pilot-fixtures/research.md:3). Among respondents, 12 requested source citations, and nine requested spreadsheet import (source://pilot-fixtures/research.md:3-4). Those groups may overlap, so the requests should not be counted as separate sets of teams (source://pilot-fixtures/research.md:4). The survey measured preferences only; it did not measure time savings, market share, revenue, or product performance (source://pilot-fixtures/research.md:5; source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:6).

For that reason, the pilot will emphasize careful evidence handling, transparent drafting, and grounded communication. We will use plain English and avoid claims that the product eliminates all errors (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:6). When the team discusses data from the pilot, illustrative information will not be described as measured product performance (source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:6). These guardrails are part of the pilot’s purpose: to learn what helps small research teams work more clearly, while keeping claims proportional to the evidence already available.

The approved launch date is November 12, 2026, and that date supersedes an archived proposal that listed October 15, 2026 (source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:4; source://pilot-fixtures/archive/old-brief.md:3-4). The archived proposal also mentioned 30 teams, but that value was not approved; the current approved pilot group remains 24 invited teams (source://pilot-fixtures/archive/old-brief.md:4; source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:5). The decision record was dated September 20, 2026 (source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:3).

During the pilot, the support owner will be the Pilot Team (source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:5). Teams should contact the Pilot Team for next steps, as indicated in the pilot update draft (source://pilot-fixtures/article.md:15-17). The budget cap for the approved pilot is USD 1,200 (source://pilot-fixtures/decisions.md:5). Export language will also remain precise: the term “download package” refers to an exported bundle, supported input formats depend on runtime capabilities, and a spreadsheet formula field should not be treated as proof of full Excel compatibility (source://pilot-fixtures/guides/exports.md:3-4).

This pilot is a practical, evidence-focused step toward understanding how Kition can support research workflows. It begins with invited teams, a bounded promise, and clear limits on what we know today (source://pilot-fixtures/brief.md:3-5; source://pilot-fixtures/research.md:5).
